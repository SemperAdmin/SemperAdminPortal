## Start Here If You Are New to SLA

Special Leave Accrual (SLA) lets you keep leave above the normal 60-day limit when duty kept you from taking it. You carry up to 30 extra days, so your total stays at or under 90 days.

SLA is not automatic. You build a short package, your commanding officer (CO) endorses it, and the first Marine general officer (GO) in your administrative chain approves it. After approval, your CO restores the days in Marine Online (MOL).

By the end of this page you will know.

- The three ways to qualify for FY26 SLA.
- What does not qualify.
- Who approves, and why nobody signs for the GO.
- What goes in your package.
- When your SLA expires.
- How SLA leave is charged.
- How to check your SLA balance.

Every fact on this page comes from MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> (3 November 2026), ALNAV 056/26<Citation source="ALNAV 056/26" /> (14 September 2026), MARADMIN 188/25<Citation source="MARADMIN 188/25" /> (10 April 2025), MCO 1050.3J<Citation source="MCO 1050.3J" />, DoWI 1327.06<Citation source="DoWI 1327.06" />, Public Law 117-263 section 632, and Title 37 U.S.C. section 501<Citation source="Title 37 U.S.C. section 501" />.

## What SLA Is

SLA is leave restored to you above the 60 days you are allowed to carry from one fiscal year to the next. It exists for one reason. Operations, a natural disaster, or a catastrophe kept you from taking leave you earned.

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 6.b, SLA does not rescue leave you lost because you did not plan your leave. If you had the chance to take leave and did not, SLA does not apply.

## The 90-Day Total Cap

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 6.c, approved SLA lets you carry up to 30 days beyond the 60-day limit.

- Up to 60 days of annual leave.
- Up to 30 days of SLA.
- 90 days total.

Leave above 90 days is lost at the end of the fiscal year. Enlisted Marines have a one-time sell-back option for the excess, covered below.

## Three Ways to Qualify for FY26

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 4.b, you qualify for FY26 SLA in one of three ways.

### 1. Operation EPIC FURY

You deployed in support of Operation EPIC FURY. ALNAV 056/26<Citation source="ALNAV 056/26" /> designates the operation for SLA under DoWI 1327.06<Citation source="DoWI 1327.06" /> paragraph 3.8.d. This category covers FY26 only.

### 2. A Designated Ship or Mobile Unit Away More Than 60 Days

Your ship or unit is on the FY26 designated units list, and it deployed or operated away from its homeport or permanent duty station (PDS) for more than 60 consecutive days in support of operations in defense of national security.

- Task-organized and temporary formations count. Marine Expeditionary Units (MEUs), Special Purpose Marine Air-Ground Task Forces (SPMAGTFs), crisis response forces, and provisional battalions or detachments formed under a deployment order are all eligible types.
- Being an eligible type is not enough. The ship or unit has to be on the list.
- You count if you were joined to the unit, or attached on temporary additional duty (TAD) under formal deployment orders, during the qualifying window.
- Your SLA covers only your verified time attached to the unit while the unit was away.

The list lives on the [Manpower Military Policy (MPO) page](https://www.manpower.marines.mil/Divisions/Plans-and-Policies/Manpower-Plans-and-Policy/Manpower-Military-Policy-MPO/) of the Manpower and Reserve Affairs website. Headquarters updates it as units are added. Your S-1 checks it against your orders.

### 3. 120 Consecutive Days in a Hostile Fire or Imminent Danger Pay Area

You served at least 120 consecutive days in a location designated for Hostile Fire Pay (HFP) or Imminent Danger Pay (IDP).

The days must run back to back. A Marine who deploys for 60 days, comes home for 30, and deploys for another 60 does not qualify. The two trips are not consecutive.

## What Does Not Qualify

- Other qualifying duty. The Secretary of the Navy has not designated any. Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 4.c, requests under this category come back to you without action before they reach Headquarters Marine Corps.
- A ship or unit missing from the designated list. Your command asks Headquarters to add it, see Where to Go for Help.
- Leave lost to poor planning. Paragraph 6.b rules it out.
- Contingency operations as a category of its own. MARADMIN 188/25 listed it. MARADMIN 545/26 does not. EPIC FURY is the one designated operation for FY26.

## Who Approves Your SLA

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 6.a, the first Marine general officer in your administrative chain of command approves SLA. Nobody below the GO approves it, and nobody signs "by direction" for the GO.

<Callout variant="info" title="Edge cases go to MPO through your S-1">
  Paragraph 6.b describes GO approval in terms of designated ships and mobile units. The EPIC FURY and hostile fire or imminent danger pay categories sit in paragraph 4.b beside it. If your S-1 or chain questions whether the GO approves your category, the S-1 takes the policy question to MPO per paragraph 9.
</Callout>

## Build Your Package

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 7.c, your package holds three items.

1. An Administrative Action Form requesting SLA.
2. Proof you were assigned to, or attached on TAD to, the qualifying unit or location during the qualifying period. Web orders, a deployment manifest, or an operational travel claim.
3. A written endorsement from your CO.

Then follow these steps.

1. Gather your orders and travel claim.
2. Fill out the Administrative Action Form with your S-1.
3. Get your CO's endorsement.
4. Your S-1 forwards the package up the administrative chain to the first Marine GO.
5. Wait for the decision. Ask your S-1 for status, not the GO's office.

## After Approval

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 7.d, the approved package goes to your unit S-1.

1. Your CO restores the approved days in MOL.
2. Your CO adds a comment naming the GO who approved and the approval date.
3. Your S-1 keeps the package under the Marine Corps Records Management Program.

Check MOL and your next Leave and Earnings Statement (LES) to confirm the days came back.

## When Your SLA Expires

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 6.c.1, SLA expires at the end of the second fiscal year for which it is approved.

### The Example From the Message

Paragraph 6.c.2 walks through it.

1. On 1 October 2026, a Marine loses 27 days. The balance drops to 60.
2. After MARADMIN 545/26 releases, the Marine submits an SLA request. The GO approves it.
3. On 30 October 2026, the CO restores the 27 days. The balance is 87.
4. The SLA is approved for FY26 and restored in FY27. It stays available until 1 October 2028.

Use FY26 SLA by 30 September 2028.

## How SLA Leave Is Charged

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 8.c and MCO 1050.3J<Citation source="MCO 1050.3J" />, leave is charged last in, first out. You spend this year's leave first. Leave carried over from prior years, including SLA, is charged only after this year's leave runs out.

Plan for it. Taking 10 days of leave does not draw down your SLA while you still hold current-year leave.

## SLA Balance Gate

Per MARADMIN 188/25<Citation source="MARADMIN 188/25" /> paragraph 7.b, you are not authorized more SLA until your SLA balance drops below 30 days. MARADMIN 545/26 does not change this rule.

## Check Your SLA Balance

Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 8.d and 8.e, check two places.

- Your LES. It shows your SLA balance and forecasts leave you are about to lose.
- MOL. Go to the Leave/Liberty tab, Leave Overview section, SLA History.

### Read Your LES

1. Pull your LES from myPay each payday.
2. Find the Leave Information section.
3. BAL is your total accrued leave.
4. USE/LOSE is leave you lose at fiscal year end if you do not take it.
5. Confirm your SLA balance and the fiscal year it expires.
6. Take anything wrong to your S-1.

## COVID-19 Transition Window Closed

MARADMIN 188/25<Citation source="MARADMIN 188/25" /> paragraph 7.a gave Marines holding COVID-era SLA above 90 days until 30 September 2026 to use it. The window closed. Nothing on this page reopens it.

## One-Time SLA Sell-Back (Enlisted Only)

Per Public Law 117-263 section 632, Title 37 U.S.C. section 501<Citation source="Title 37 U.S.C. section 501" />, and MARADMIN 188/25<Citation source="MARADMIN 188/25" /> paragraph 8, enlisted Marines losing leave above 90 days sell back up to 30 days of it, one time.

- Officers are not eligible. The law limits it to enlisted Marines.
- The leave above 90 days must be leave you would have kept as SLA if not for the 90-day limit.
- Submit the election through MOL with your supporting documents.
- You make this election once in your career.
- The days count toward your 60-day career sell-back limit. The Annual Leave page covers the career limit.

## Common Questions Marines Ask

I lost leave on 1 October 2026. Is it gone?

Not if you qualify. Per MARADMIN 545/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 545/26" /> paragraph 6.c.2, lost FY26 leave comes back after an approved request. Build your package now.

My SLA request came back without action this summer. Do I start over?

Ask your S-1 first. Per paragraph 7.a, units review every pending, held, and returned package against the designated units list. If yours qualifies, it moves forward.

I was on a MEU for 7 months. Do I qualify?

Only if your MEU is on the FY26 designated units list. MEUs are an eligible type under paragraph 3.b, and the unit still has to be designated. Your S-1 checks the list.

I was attached on TAD to a designated unit. Do I count?

Yes, if you were attached under formal deployment orders during the qualifying window. Per paragraph 3.c, your SLA covers only your verified time with the unit while it was away.

I served 5 months in an imminent danger pay area. Do I qualify?

Yes. 5 months clears the 120 consecutive day threshold in paragraph 4.b.3. Build your package with your orders.

My unit is not on the list. What now?

Tell your chain of command. Per paragraph 8.a, your command sends a request to add the unit, endorsed by a general officer, to the Director, Manpower Plans and Policy Division. If it is approved, the list updates.

Who approves my SLA?

The first Marine general officer in your administrative chain. No one signs for the GO.

When does my FY26 SLA expire?

Use it by 30 September 2028.

I have 30 days of SLA already. Do I get more?

No. Per MARADMIN 188/25<Citation source="MARADMIN 188/25" /> paragraph 7.b, you get no new SLA until your balance drops below 30 days.

Do officers get the one-time sell-back?

No. It is for enlisted Marines only.
