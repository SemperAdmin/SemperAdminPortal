## Why JEPES Exists

The Pro and Con marks system was a holdover from a different era. Two single numbers told the Marine and the section nothing about the work performed across distinct lanes. JEPES splits performance into evaluable categories with observable behavioral anchors. Done right, the Marine sees the path to a higher mark next cycle. Done wrong, JEPES becomes the new Pro and Con.

## Who Evaluates Whom

Per MCO 1616.1<Citation source="MCO 1616.1" /> Chapter 2 paragraph 1, the reporting chain runs First Line Supervisor, Evaluator, Reviewer, then Approver. The First Line Supervisor has direct supervision and recommends the marks. The Approver, an O-5 level commander or OIC equivalent, approves the final marks. The order sets no required rank or billet for any role. The section leader owns the cadence and quality.

If you cannot observe a Marine's performance directly across the cycle, you are not the right writer. Surface that to the chain before the report drops.

## What JEPES Evaluates

Per MCO 1616.1<Citation source="MCO 1616.1" /> Chapter 1 paragraph 1.c(1), the PES score has four equally weighted pillars, each worth 25 percent.

- Warfighting. Rifle score percentile and MCMAP belt.
- Physical Toughness. PFT and CFT score percentiles.
- Mental Agility. Informal PME in grade and civilian self-education.
- Command Input. Average marks across three lines of subjective evaluation.

For each category, the writer assigns a mark anchored to a behavioral description, not a memory of the cycle's most recent event.

## Cadence

Per MCO 1616.1<Citation source="MCO 1616.1" /> Chapter 2 paragraph 2, active component Marines complete a semi-annual evaluation every six months, February through July and August through January. Reserve component Marines complete an annual evaluation. Every active JEPES Marine follows the same semi-annual cycle.

The order requires the First Line Supervisor to give an initial written counseling within 30 days of the relationship starting. Tie your JEPES sit-down to your counseling cadence so the two become one conversation.

## How JEPES Connects to Promotions

Per MCO 1616.1<Citation source="MCO 1616.1" /> Appendix C paragraph 1, JEPES PES scores drive the monthly competition for promotion to Corporal and Sergeant. Promotion to PFC and LCpl does not use JEPES. The system replaced the role Pro and Con marks held in junior enlisted promotion. Promotion is a monthly competition on the PES score across four pillars, and the rifle score is one part of it. A Marine with weak JEPES results across consecutive cycles lowers his or her competitive position.

## Pitfalls Specific to JEPES

- Mark inflation. Every Marine cannot be above standard. The category anchors mean what they say.
- Mark deflation. Punishing the section through low marks teaches the wrong lesson and hides genuine performance issues from the chain.
- Surprising the Marine. The JEPES result sits inside the counseling cadence. The Marine should never see a number without context.
- Skipping the conversation. JEPES is a leader development tool, not a paperwork drill. The mark exists to drive the next conversation.
