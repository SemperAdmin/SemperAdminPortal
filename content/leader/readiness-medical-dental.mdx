## What the IMR Categories Mean

Per DoDI 6025.19<Citation source="DoDI 6025.19" /> paragraph 3.1.a, every Marine sits in one Individual Medical Readiness (IMR) category in the service tracking system. The category summarizes the Marine's PHA, dental readiness assessment, immunizations, lab studies, medical equipment, and deployability.

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| FMR | Fully Medically Ready. PHA and dental assessment current, DRC 1 or 2, immunizations, labs, and equipment complete, deployable |
| PMR | Partially Medically Ready. Overdue PHA or dental assessment (DRC 4), or missing immunizations, labs, or equipment |
| NMR | Not Medically Ready. Deployment-limiting medical condition, or DRC 3 urgent dental need |

A section full of FMR Marines deploys on alert. A section with PMR and NMR Marines starts behind.

## Monthly Section Pull

Pull the IMR category for every Marine on a fixed monthly date.

- Identify NMR Marines. These are deploy-blockers.
- Identify PMR Marines. These are overdue or missing a required element.
- Identify PMR Marines with appointments scheduled. Track to closure.
- Identify the action item for each below-standard Marine.

Common actions.

- Schedule the PHA (annual).
- Schedule dental cleaning or treatment.
- Update immunizations.
- Coordinate medical appointment for the limiting condition.
- Coordinate lab work.

## Dental Class Discipline

Dental Readiness Class (DRC) drives a sub-component of the IMR category. Per DoDI 6025.19 paragraph 3.5.b.

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DRC 1 | Current exam, no treatment needed. FMR |
| DRC 2 | Current exam, non-urgent treatment needed. FMR |
| DRC 3 | Urgent or emergent treatment needed. NMR, temporary non-deployable |
| DRC 4 | Overdue for the annual dental readiness assessment. PMR |

A Marine in DRC 3 cannot deploy until treatment completes. Treatment takes weeks. The section catches DRC 3 monthly, not at the alert.

## Reserve Component Considerations

Reserve Marines schedule their own medical and dental through Reserve Health Readiness or coordinate with civilian providers.

- Brief reserve Marines on the requirements.
- Coordinate Reserve Health Readiness Program enrollment.
- Track civilian provider coordination if the Marine pursues it.
- Verify the status update reaches the readiness system.

The leader cannot drive the appointment scheduling for reserve Marines the same way as active. The leader can drive the awareness and the follow-up.

## Tracking the Section

Maintain a section readiness sheet.

| Marine | IMR | Dental | Last PHA | Action | Due Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Review weekly. A Marine sitting at NMR or DRC 3 for two months is a Marine the section is not pushing.

## Briefing the Commander

Monthly section IMR and dental status brief.

- Total Marines.
- FMR count.
- NMR and DRC 3 count with action plan.
- PMR and DRC 4 count with appointment scheduled.
- Trend over the prior 90 days.

Commander wants the trend, not just the snapshot.

## When the Deployment Alert Drops

A deployed-tomorrow alert with NMR Marines is a section in trouble.

- Some DRC 3 conditions resolve fast. Schedule today.
- Some DRC 3 conditions take weeks (extractions, implants). The Marine likely cannot deploy.
- Some deployment-limiting conditions require waivers. Coordinate with medical.

The fix is to never let NMR or DRC 3 sit unaddressed in the first place.

## Common Tracking Failures

- Monthly pull skipped during a busy month.
- PMR and DRC 4 Marines ignored because "they will get to it."
- Reserve Marines fall through the cracks.
- Treatment tracked but post-appointment update never verified.
- No commander brief, so the chain finds out at the alert.
