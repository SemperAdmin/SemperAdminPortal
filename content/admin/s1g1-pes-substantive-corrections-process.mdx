## Role and responsibility

Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10 paragraph 3a(1), only the Marine and appointed counsel petition to change the record. Third parties, including reporting officials, do not. The Marine submits the DD 149 to CMC (MMRP-13). The S-1 officer assists only. The S-1 chief helps build the supporting package. The PES clerk pulls the filed report. The PERB hears fitness report petitions first.

Source documents flow from the filed fitness report, the identified error, supporting documentation, and the Marine's DD 149. Correction actions land at CMC (MMRP-13) and go to the PERB. Outputs include the PERB decision, the OMPF update on approval, the unit PES register update, and the response to the Marine.

Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10 (Substantive Corrections Process), substantive corrections apply where errors affect the substance of the fitness report. Administrative corrections covering clerical errors follow a separate path.

## Trigger events

- Marine identifies substantive error in a filed fitness report.
- RS or RO identifies an error after submission. The RS or RO does not file. The Marine decides whether to petition.
- An OMPF audit or MMSB return shows a discrepancy. The S-1 tells the Marine, and the Marine files the DD 149 if a petition is warranted.

## Processing workflow

1. Identify the correction need. Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10, identify the substantive error.
2. Determine scope. Substantive (content affecting performance evaluation) versus administrative (clerical) per Chapter 10.
3. Help the Marine build the DD 149 package. Stage the original report, the identified error, the supporting documentation, and the proposed correction.
4. Capture supporting documentation. Gather witness statements, command memoranda, and corroborating records.
5. Skip chain endorsement. Per Chapter 10 paragraph 3a(2), the Marine submits the DD 149 directly. No chain endorsement applies.
6. Marine submits to CMC (MMRP-13). Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10 paragraph 3a(3), the Marine emails the documents and images in .pdf format or mails them. Do not submit through PES.
7. Track PERB review. Help the Marine check status on a fixed cycle.
8. Process the PERB decision. The PERB grants or disapproves. Per paragraph 4d, a disapproval forwards to the BCNR for final determination. On approval, update OMPF.
9. Update OMPF on approval. Per MCO P1070.12K<Citation source="MCO P1070.12K" />, the corrected report posts to OMPF.
10. Document the action. Update the PES register with correction date, PERB decision, and outcome.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- PES (HQMC Performance Evaluation System). Source of the filed report. Not the petition path.
- PERB (Performance Evaluation Review Board). Hears fitness report petitions first. MMSB does not adjudicate.
- OMPF. Final record updated on approval.
- BCNR. Board for Correction of Naval Records. Receives PERB disapprovals and petitions on records other than fitness reports.

### Forms

- DD Form 149. Application for correction, filed by the Marine per Chapter 10 paragraph 3a.
- Original Fitness Report Copy. Source document.
- Supporting Documentation Package. Witness statements and corroborating records.

## Common pitfalls

- Administrative correction routed as substantive. Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10, scope determines routing. Validate before submission.
- Supporting documentation incomplete. The petition lacks support. Stage full documentation before submission.
- Petition routed through the chain. Per Chapter 10 paragraph 3a(2), the Marine files directly with CMC (MMRP-13). No chain endorsement applies.
- OMPF not updated on approval. The correction sits unposted. Verify OMPF update after PERB approval.
- BCNR routing missed when applicable. Per Chapter 10 paragraph 5b, the PERB hears fitness report petitions first. Records other than fitness reports go to the BCNR only.

## Decision points

- Substantive versus administrative. Per MCO 1610.7B<Citation source="MCO 1610.7B" /> Chapter 10, substantive errors affect evaluation content. The S-1 officer advises the Marine on scope. The order assigns no scope validation to the S-1.
- PERB versus BCNR routing. Per Chapter 10 paragraph 5b, the PERB hears fitness report petitions first. A PERB disapproval forwards to the BCNR. Records other than fitness reports go to the BCNR only. MMSB does not adjudicate.
- Endorsement scope. No chain endorsement applies. The Marine files directly and the S-1 officer assists only.
