## Role and responsibility

The lead command DCP runs the joint directive process per MCO 5215.1K<Citation source="MCO 5215.1K" /> Enclosure (1) Chapter 4 paragraph 1 (Introduction). The S-1 chief tracks coordination through preliminary (Chapter 4 paragraph 7) and final (Chapter 4 paragraph 8) phases. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 9, CMC, ACMC and the Deputy Commandants sign HQMC joint directives, and field command directives are signed as delegated by the senior principal official. The signature block follows Chapter 4 paragraph 10. Every partner command's DCP indexes the joint directive on issuance.

Source documents flow from the policy or procedural area requiring multi-principal action, the partner command identification, the preliminary coordination feedback, the final coordination signatures, the SSIC assignment per Chapter 4 paragraph 2, and the applicability scope per Chapter 4 paragraph 3. Joint directive outputs include the signed joint directive with the multi-principal signature block per Chapter 4 paragraph 10, the checklist of effective joint directives per Chapter 4 paragraph 6, the DCP index entries at every partner command, and the joint cancellation when applicable per Chapter 4 paragraph 4.

Per MCO 5215.1K Enclosure (1) Chapter 4 paragraph 1 (Introduction), the primary principal coordinates and signs a joint directive by default. Joint signature applies only where the subject matter divides or overlaps between principals. Per paragraph 2, only the SSIC of the primary principal shows on the directive. Per paragraph 3 (Applicability), a joint directive applies to all addressees by distribution list and Publications Control Number, the same as a regular directive.

## Trigger events

- Multi-command or multi-service policy or procedural area requires unified directive.
- Marine Corps and Navy area requires joint directive (e.g., naval-Marine joint policy).
- Marine Corps and another agency partnership requires formal directive.
- Existing joint directive expires or requires update.
- Cancellation of a joint directive routed for processing.
- Coordination breakdown identified during internal review.

## Processing workflow

1. Validate joint directive applicability. Per MCO 5215.1K<Citation source="MCO 5215.1K" /> Enclosure (1) Chapter 4 paragraph 3 (Applicability of Joint Directives), confirm the distribution list and Publications Control Number reach all addressees, the same as a regular directive.
2. Apply SSIC and signature requirements. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 2 (SSIC and Signature Requirements), assign the primary principal SSIC and confirm whether the subject matter calls for joint signature.
3. Build the joint directive content. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 5 (Information Required for Joint Directives), obtain in writing from each participating principal the office code, the signer title, the distribution codes and the print procurement method.
4. Maintain the checklist of effective joint directives. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 6 (Checklist of Effective Directives), list the joint directive in the primary principal's checklist. The joint directive also appears in the checklists of other participating principals as needed.
5. Run preliminary coordination. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 7 (Preliminary Coordination), a joint working group normally does the preliminary work. The coordinator of the primary principal prepares the draft for working group review.
6. Run final coordination. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 8 (Final Coordination), the lead DCP circulates the final draft for partner command signature.
7. Apply signature authority. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 8b, obtain the signature of the cognizant principal subordinate if a higher common superior does not sign. Per paragraph 9 (Signature Authority), CMC, ACMC and the Deputy Commandants sign HQMC joint directives. Field command directives are signed as delegated by the senior principal official.
8. Apply two-or-more-principal format. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 10 (Format of a Directive Signed by Two or More Principals), the signature block accommodates every signing principal.
9. Process cancellation. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 4 (Canceling Joint Directives), the primary principal obtains concurrence from all participating principals before cancellation.
10. Distribute the joint directive. Route through the lead DCP and each partner command's DCP per the distribution list.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- Lead command DCP. Runs the joint directive process.
- Partner command DCPs. Index the joint directive on signature.
- HQMC Directives Portal. Surfaces Marine Corps-signed joint directives.
- DON Directives Issuance System. Surfaces Navy and DON-level joint directives.
- Checklist of Effective Joint Directives. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 6.

### Forms

- Signer Brief. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 8c, prepare and attach a brief to the directive for the signer.
- Joint signature block. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 10, follow the joint letter-type order format. No separate form applies.

## Common pitfalls

- Preliminary coordination skipped or rushed. Per MCO 5215.1K<Citation source="MCO 5215.1K" /> Enclosure (1) Chapter 4 paragraph 7, the preliminary coordination identifies partner command objections early. Skipping the phase risks a stalled final coordination.
- Signature rule misapplied. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 8b, obtain the signature of the cognizant principal subordinate if a higher common superior does not sign.
- Cancellation processed without partner concurrence. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 4, the primary principal obtains concurrence from all participating principals and the other participants justify any continued need. Unilateral cancellation breaks the joint authority.
- Partner SSIC shown on the directive. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 2, only the SSIC of the primary principal appears.
- Applicability paragraph excludes a signing principal command. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 3, applicability follows the distribution list and Publications Control Number. Validate the distribution at draft.

## Decision points

- Joint directive versus separate single-principal directives. Per MCO 5215.1K<Citation source="MCO 5215.1K" /> Enclosure (1) Chapter 4 paragraphs 1 and 3, when the same policy applies to multiple principals with coordinated implementation, the joint directive is the right path. Separate directives suit independent implementation.
- Lead command selection. The lead command runs the DCP process. The partner commands signal preliminary and final coordination.
- Cancellation routing path. Per Chapter 4 paragraph 4, the primary principal must obtain concurrence from all participating principals before cancellation. The partners justify any continued need.
