## Role and responsibility

The convening authority (CA) convenes the Type Two Litigation-Report Investigation per JAGINST 5800.7G<Citation source="JAGINST 5800.7G" /> Chapter II section 0210.c (Convening Order) using Appendix A-2-f (Sample Litigation-Report Investigation Convening Order). The investigating officer (IO) executes per section 0210.d (Investigatory Approach). The legal advisor reviews for sufficiency per section 0210.g (Review) with special attention to attorney work-product and attorney-client privilege protections distinguishing Type Two from Type One. The CA acts per section 0210.f (CA Action). The Navy or DON litigation authority receives the report for use in claims and litigation per the purpose stated in section 0210.a.

Source documents flow from the preliminary inquiry per JAGMAN 0203 with special attention to section 0203.d (Preliminary inquiries into incidents involving potential claims or litigation), the command-options decision per 0204, the Type Two convening order per Appendix A-2-f, the witness statements and evidence per JAGMAN 0207, the IO findings and analysis structured per Appendix A-2-g, and the legal sufficiency review. Type Two outputs include the certified Litigation-Report Investigation per Appendix A-2-g, the CA endorsement, the legal sufficiency review, the case file routed to OJAG Code 15 via the GCMCA staff judge advocate per section 0210.f(2), and release controlled by OJAG Code 15 per section 0210.h. Appendix A-2-m covers Claims for or Against the Government.

Per JAGMAN section 0210.a (Purpose), the Type Two Litigation-Report Investigation gathers facts and develops the government's litigation position for matters with potential government liability. Per section 0210.b (General), the investigation enjoys attorney work-product and attorney-client privilege protections distinct from Type One. Per section 0210.h (Release), release rules differ from Type One per the litigation-privilege framework.

## Trigger events

- Preliminary inquiry per JAGMAN 0203.d identifies potential claims or litigation.
- Vehicle accident with civilian damage or injury.
- Government property loss or damage involving third-party claim.
- Aviation mishap with civilian impact per Appendix A-2-n.
- Maritime incident with potential claim per Appendix A-2-o, A-2-p, or A-2-q.
- Tort claim filed against the government.
- Government claim against a third party.
- Sonic boom or noise claim per Appendix A-2-w.
- Do not use Type Two for an active duty death, for a civilian death while accompanying military personnel in the field or from military-related actions, or for a major incident. Per JAGMAN 0210.a(1)-(2), those cases follow other investigation types.

## Processing workflow

1. Identify litigation or claims potential. Per JAGINST 5800.7G<Citation source="JAGINST 5800.7G" /> Chapter II section 0203.d (Preliminary Inquiries into Incidents Involving Potential Claims or Litigation), the preliminary inquiry surfaces the claims or litigation posture.
2. Convene Type Two. Per JAGMAN section 0210.c (Convening Order) and Appendix A-2-f, the CA convenes Type Two with an order naming the supervisory judge advocate and stating the investigation is convened and the report prepared in contemplation of litigation, to assist attorneys representing the United States. The order sets a completion date, normally within 30 calendar days, and does not direct opinions or recommendations. The sample order is Appendix A-2-f.
3. Apply the investigatory approach. Per JAGMAN section 0210.d (Investigatory Approach), the IO gathers evidence under the direction of the supervisory judge advocate named in the convening order. The IO reports to this judge advocate before collecting any evidence per section 0210.c(1). The IO does not take written, signed, or recorded witness statements and instead summarizes each interview for the supervisory judge advocate per section 0210.d(2). A claimant or opposing party willing to sign a statement is the exception, after the IO consults the supervisory judge advocate.
4. Build the Litigation-Report investigation. Per JAGMAN section 0210.e (Litigation-Report Investigation) and Appendix A-2-g (Sample Litigation-Report Investigation), structure the report. The format closely tracks JAGMAN 0208 with Type Two-specific elements supporting the litigation posture.
5. Apply attorney-work-product and attorney-client protections. Per JAGMAN section 0210, the Litigation-Report enjoys protections from disclosure to opposing parties in litigation. The legal advisor briefs the IO on protected versus discoverable content.
6. Route to CA action. Per JAGMAN section 0210.f (CA Action), the CA endorses the report in writing within 30 calendar days or returns it to the supervisory judge advocate for further inquiry. The CA does not normally approve or disapprove the findings of fact.
7. Process review. Per JAGMAN section 0210.g (Review), the legal advisor reviews for sufficiency, the litigation-privilege framework, and the claims posture.
8. Handle release. Per JAGMAN section 0210.h (Release of Litigation-Report Investigations), OJAG Code 15 is the sole release and denial authority. Convening and reviewing commands do not release the report or its contents. Release rules differ from Type One.
9. Cross-reference Appendix A-2-m. Per Appendix A-2-m (Claims for or Against the Government), the claims framework applies when a claim is filed or anticipated.
10. Route the case file to OJAG Code 15. Per section 0210.f(2), the CA keeps a copy and forwards the original plus one copy to OJAG Code 15 via the staff judge advocate of the GCMCA. Claims and litigation staff use the report per the purpose in section 0210.a.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- Convening Authority Legal Records. Holds the certified Litigation-Report per MCO 5800.16 Volume 1.
- Investigating Officer Working File. Holds the case file with privilege markings.
- OJAG Code 15 Investigations Branch. Receives the original plus one copy per JAGMAN 0210.f(2).
- Staff Judge Advocate of the GCMCA. Reviews the report and forwards it within 30 calendar days per JAGMAN 0210.g(2).
- Litigation File. Privileged file held under legal advisor control.

## Common pitfalls

- Type Two convened without litigation or claims trigger. Per JAGINST 5800.7G<Citation source="JAGINST 5800.7G" /> Chapter II sections 0203.d and 0210.a, Type Two attaches to matters with litigation or claims potential. Convening Type Two on a routine Type One matter wastes the litigation-privilege framework.
- Attorney work-product protections not marked. Per JAGMAN section 0210, the privileged content marks. Unmarked content risks disclosure to opposing parties.
- Witness warning skipped. Per JAGMAN section 0207.c (cross-reference), the witness warning rules apply across investigation types.
- Command releases the report. Per JAGMAN section 0210.h, convening and reviewing commands do not release a litigation-report investigation or its contents. Send release requests to OJAG Code 15.
- Case file routing delayed. Per JAGMAN 0210.f(2), OJAG Code 15 receives the original plus one copy. A delayed routing slows claims processing.
- Type Two report structure copies Type One format without litigation-specific sections. Per Appendix A-2-g, the Litigation-Report uses a distinct sample tailored to the litigation purpose.

## Decision points

- Type Two versus Type One routing. Per JAGINST 5800.7G<Citation source="JAGINST 5800.7G" /> Chapter II sections 0204 and 0210.a, the CA routes to Type Two when litigation or claims posture surfaces. Type One suits matters without the posture.
- Attorney work-product scope. Per JAGMAN section 0210, the legal advisor briefs the IO on protected versus discoverable content. The IO marks per the brief.
- Release scope. Per JAGMAN section 0210.h, OJAG Code 15 is the sole release and denial authority. Commands do not release the report or its contents.
- Case file routing path. Per JAGMAN 0210.f(2), the CA routes the original plus one copy to OJAG Code 15 via the staff judge advocate of the GCMCA.
