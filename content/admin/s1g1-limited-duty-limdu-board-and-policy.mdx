## Role and responsibility

The medical board at the medical treatment facility (MTF) generates the LIMDU recommendation per MCO 1900.16<Citation source="MCO 1900.16" /> Chapter 8 paragraph 8104 (Limited Duty Overview). The MTF commander recommends assignment to a period of TLD per paragraph 8110.2.a (Limited Duty Responsibilities). The MEB approves enlisted TLD up to 12 months. Officer TLD and enlisted TLD past 12 months go to CMC (MMSR-4). TLD status comes from the medical board action. The unit commander applies the duty restrictions and reports duty limitation code Q in MCTFS for a Marine in a bona fide TLD status per paragraph 8110.1.b.(7)(b). The S-1 chief tracks the LIMDU period and the re-evaluation gate per paragraph 8110. The S-1 officer signs by direction on the LIMDU transaction routing. The IPAC outbound counselor cuts the duty status transaction in MCTFS through UD-MIPS per MCTFSPRIUM<Citation source="MCTFSPRIUM" />.

Source documents flow from the medical condition, the LIMDU board package, the MTF TLD recommendation, the duty restrictions list, the LIMDU duration recommendation, and the re-evaluation appointment per paragraph 8110. LIMDU outputs include the LIMDU duty status from the medical board action, the documented LIMDU period (up to two 6 month periods, 12 months cumulative for enlisted at the MTF), the unit LIMDU tracker entry, the re-evaluation gate schedule, the medical extension if recommended per paragraph 8111, and either the fit-for-full-duty return or the DES referral per Chapter 8 Section 2.

Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8102 (Purpose), LIMDU exists to allow a Marine to recover from a medical condition while remaining on active duty. Per paragraph 8104 (Limited Duty Overview), LIMDU runs when the medical officer expects the Marine to return to full duty within the LIMDU period. Per paragraphs 8003.24 and 8102, TLD runs in up to two 6 month periods, 12 months cumulative for enlisted at the MTF. A MEB goes to the PEB when the Marine is unlikely to return to full duty within the 12 month cumulative period. The paragraph 8111 medical extension extends the contract, not the duty period.

## Trigger events

- MTF medical board recommends LIMDU after a covered medical condition.
- Light duty period exceeds the threshold triggering LIMDU referral.
- Marine returns from convalescent leave with continued medical limitation.
- LIMDU board package routes through the MTF commander.
- TLD re-evaluation opens 60 days before the TLD period ends.
- 12 month cumulative LIMDU duration approaches the MEB and DES referral point.
- MTF recommends further TLD or DES referral at re-evaluation.

## Processing workflow

1. Receive the MTF LIMDU board recommendation. The MTF medical board generates the recommendation with the medical scope, the expected recovery time, and the recommended duty restrictions.
2. Validate the LIMDU period. Per MCO 1900.16<Citation source="MCO 1900.16" /> Chapter 8 paragraph 8105 (Limited Duty Policy), TLD runs in up to two 6 month periods, 12 months cumulative for enlisted at the MTF, per paragraphs 8003.24 and 8102. The paragraph 8111 medical extension extends the contract, not the duty period.
3. Route the MTF recommendation. Per paragraph 8110.2.a, the MTF commander recommends assignment to a period of TLD.
4. Unit commander applies restrictions. The medical board action sets TLD status. The unit commander applies the duty restrictions per the medical recommendation and reports duty limitation code Q.
5. Cut the LIMDU duty status transaction. Per Appendix E (Automated Processing under MCTFS) and MCTFSPRIUM<Citation source="MCTFSPRIUM" />, the IPAC cuts the LIMDU transaction in UD-MIPS.
6. Record locally. MCO P1070.12K<Citation source="MCO P1070.12K" /> prescribes no LIMDU Page 11 entry. Record the LIMDU period, the medical scope, the duty restrictions, and the re-evaluation gate in the unit LIMDU tracker.
7. Track the LIMDU re-evaluation gate. Per paragraph 8110 (Limited Duty Responsibilities), the unit IPAC and the S-1 chief monitor the re-evaluation appointment cycle, 60 days before each TLD period ends.
8. Coordinate referral to DES. Per Chapter 8 Section 2 paragraphs 8201-8208 and DODI 1332.18, the MTF refers to the Disability Evaluation System when the medical condition warrants PEB review.
9. Process LIMDU extension. Per paragraph 8111.1.a, the commanding officer approves the first medical extension on the NAVMC 321M and CMC (MMSR-4) approves later ones. The extension covers the TLD period up to 6 months.
10. Close LIMDU. On fit-for-full-duty return, the unit closes the LIMDU duty status in MCTFS. On DES referral, the LIMDU status transitions per Section 2.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- Marine Corps Total Force System (MCTFS). Holds LIMDU duty status.
- UD-MIPS. Cuts LIMDU transactions per Appendix E.
- Disability Evaluation System (IDES) Workflow. Per Chapter 8 Section 2.
- MTF Records. Hold the LIMDU board package.
- Unit LIMDU Roster. Local register tracking LIMDU Marines.

### Forms

- LIMDU Board Package. Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8102 and 8104.
- MTF TLD Recommendation. Per paragraph 8110.2.a.
- Unit LIMDU tracker. Local record of the LIMDU period and the re-evaluation gate.
- LIMDU Re-Evaluation Appointment Memorandum. Per paragraph 8110.
- Medical Extension Request. Per paragraph 8111.

## Common pitfalls

- LIMDU period set past 12 months cumulative without CMC approval. Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8003.24.a, enlisted TLD runs up to 12 months at the MTF. Periods past 12 months need CMC (MMSR-4) approval, or referral to the PEB when no return to full duty is expected. Section 2 applies.
- MTF TLD recommendation missing. Per paragraph 8110.2.a, the MTF commander recommends assignment to a period of TLD.
- Re-evaluation gate missed. Per paragraph 8110, the unit tracks re-evaluation 60 days before each TLD period ends.
- LIMDU duty status not cut in MCTFS. Per Appendix E and MCTFSPRIUM, the IPAC cuts the transaction.
- LIMDU action not tracked. Record every LIMDU period and re-evaluation date in the unit LIMDU tracker.
- DES referral skipped on PEB-warranting findings. Per Chapter 8 Section 2 paragraphs 8201-8208, PEB-warranting medical conditions refer to DES.
- LIMDU continued past extension without re-evaluation. Per paragraph 8111.1.a.(3), the extension should not exceed 6 months for TLD and 12 months for a Marine pending DES referral or PEB determination. Past the limit, the Marine returns to CMC (MMSR-4) for a modification.
- Duty limitation code Q not reported. Per paragraph 8110.1.b.(7)(b), the unit commander reports code Q for every Marine in a bona fide TLD status.

## Decision points

- LIMDU versus DES referral. Per paragraphs 8104 and Section 2, LIMDU runs when the Marine is expected to return to full duty within the LIMDU period. DES runs when the condition warrants PEB review.
- One TLD period versus 12 months cumulative. Per paragraphs 8003.24 and 8102, TLD runs in up to two 6 month periods. The paragraph 8111 extension extends the contract, not the duty period.
- Fit-for-full-duty return versus extension. The MTF medical board recommends. The MEB approves enlisted TLD up to 12 months.
- DES referral timing. Per Section 2 and DODI 1332.18, the referral runs when the medical condition does not support a fit-for-full-duty return within the LIMDU period.
