## Role and responsibility

The medical treatment facility (MTF) medical officer issues the light duty or LIMDU recommendation per MCO 1900.16<Citation source="MCO 1900.16" /> Chapter 8 Section 1 paragraphs 8101-8111. The MTF commander recommends assignment to a period of TLD per paragraph 8110.2.a (Limited Duty Responsibilities). The MEB approves enlisted TLD up to 12 months. Officer TLD and enlisted TLD past 12 months go to CMC (MMSR-4). The commanding officer approves the first medical extension on the NAVMC 321M per paragraph 8111.1.a, and CMC (MMSR-4) approves later extensions. CMC (MMSR-4) approves Permanent Limited Duty (PLD) per paragraph 8108 and Expanded Permanent Limited Duty (EPLD) per paragraph 8109 for Marines the Physical Evaluation Board (PEB) found unfit. CMC decides EPLD case by case. The unit commander applies the duty restrictions, reports duty limitation code Q, and tracks LIMDU compliance. The S-1 chief tracks every Marine on light duty, LIMDU, PLD, or EPLD against the re-evaluation gate. The IPAC outbound counselor cuts the duty status transaction in MCTFS through UD-MIPS per MCTFSPRIUM<Citation source="MCTFSPRIUM" />. Only CMC (MMSR-4) enters or removes PLD codes per paragraph 8110.3.e. The commanding officer decides TLD deployability per paragraph 8107, and Marines on TLD do not execute PCS orders unless the 8107.2 conditions are met.

Source documents flow from the medical condition, the medical officer chit (light duty per paragraph 8103) or the LIMDU board package (LIMDU per paragraph 8104), the MTF TLD recommendation, the unit commander duty restriction list, the PFT or CFT authorization per paragraph 8106, the deployability and PCS restriction per paragraph 8107, the re-evaluation appointment per paragraph 8110, the medical extension recommendation per paragraph 8111 when warranted, and the DES referral package per Chapter 8 Section 2 when PEB review applies. Outputs include the duty status, the duty restrictions, the operational restrictions, the documented LIMDU period, the unit LIMDU tracker entry, the monthly LIMDU posture brief to the commander, the fit-for-full-duty return, the medical extension when applicable, the PLD or EPLD retention when the Marine requests it after PEB unfit findings and CMC (MMSR-4) approves, and the DES referral when the medical condition warrants PEB review.

Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8104 (Limited Duty Overview), LIMDU runs when the medical officer expects the Marine to return to full duty within the LIMDU period. Per paragraphs 8003.24 and 8102, TLD runs in up to two 6 month periods, 12 months cumulative for enlisted at the MTF, and a MEB goes to the PEB when the Marine is unlikely to return to full duty within the 12 month cumulative period. The paragraph 8111 medical extension extends the contract, not the duty period. Per paragraph 8108, PLD retains a Marine the PEB found unfit until a specified date. Per paragraph 8109, EPLD retains combat wounded or injured Marines the PEB found unfit.

## Sub-pages in this category

- Expanded Permanent Limited Duty. Paragraph 8109 covering EPLD retention with broader medical restrictions.
- Light Duty. Paragraph 8103 covering short-duration medical accommodation without LIMDU board.
- LIMDU Board and Policy. Paragraphs 8102, 8104, and 8105 covering LIMDU purpose, overview, and policy with up to two 6 month TLD periods, 12 months cumulative.
- Medical Extension. Paragraph 8111 covering the contract extension for TLD (up to 6 months), DES or PEB cases (up to 12 months), and short-term care (up to 2 months).
- Operational Restrictions. Paragraphs 8106 and 8107 covering partial or no PFT authorization, deployability holds, and PCS restrictions.
- Permanent Limited Duty. Paragraph 8108 covering PLD retention until a specified date for a Marine the PEB found unfit.
- Unit Responsibilities and Re-Evaluation. Paragraph 8110 covering unit, IPAC, MTF, and Marine duties tracking LIMDU duty status and re-evaluation gates.

## Trigger events

- Medical officer issues light duty recommendation.
- MTF medical board recommends LIMDU after a covered medical condition.
- LIMDU re-evaluation arrives with continued medical limitation requiring medical extension.
- PEB finds the Marine unfit and CMC (MMSR-4) approves PLD or EPLD.
- LIMDU period nears 12 months cumulative triggering MEB and DES referral.
- Marine on LIMDU faces deployment notification triggering paragraph 8107.
- PCS orders cut on a Marine on LIMDU.
- PFT or CFT cycle opens with a Marine on LIMDU.
- PLD over 12 months reaches reevaluation per paragraph 8108.8.
- IGMC FA inspection covers LIMDU posture.

## Processing workflow

1. Receive the medical recommendation. The MTF medical officer or the medical board recommends light duty (paragraph 8103) or LIMDU (paragraph 8104). Pull from the chain of command.
2. Validate the recommendation. Per MCO 1900.16<Citation source="MCO 1900.16" /> Chapter 8 paragraphs 8103 and 8104, the medical scope and the expected recovery time drive the path (light duty for short duration, LIMDU for longer cases).
3. Route the MTF recommendation. Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8110.2.a, the MTF commander recommends assignment to a period of TLD. The commanding officer approves the first medical extension on the NAVMC 321M per paragraph 8111.1.a, and CMC (MMSR-4) approves later extensions.
4. Apply duty and operational restrictions. Per paragraphs 8106 (partial or no PFT) and 8107 (deployability and PCS), the unit commander applies the operational restrictions.
5. Cut MCTFS duty status transaction. Per Appendix E and MCTFSPRIUM, the IPAC cuts the duty status transaction in UD-MIPS on entry, change, and closure.
6. Record locally. MCO P1070.12K<Citation source="MCO P1070.12K" /> prescribes no LIMDU Page 11 entry. Record the LIMDU period, the medical scope, the duty restrictions, and the re-evaluation gate in the unit LIMDU tracker.
7. Monitor re-evaluation gates. Per Chapter 8 paragraph 8110, the unit and the IPAC track the re-evaluation appointment cycle (light duty per medical chit, TLD re-evaluation 60 days before each TLD period ends, and for PLD over 12 months a complete MEB to the PEB no later than 4 months before PLD expires per paragraph 8108.8).
8. Process medical extension. Per paragraph 8111.1.a(3), the extension covers the TLD period up to 6 months, DES or PEB cases up to 12 months, and short-term care up to 2 months.
9. Process PLD and EPLD retention. Per paragraphs 8108 and 8109, the PEB unfit findings and CMC (MMSR-4) approval drive PLD or EPLD status. Apply expanded duty restrictions.
10. Refer to DES on PEB-warranting findings. Per Chapter 8 Section 2 paragraphs 8201-8208 and DODI 1332.18, the MTF refers to the Disability Evaluation System when the medical condition warrants PEB review.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- Marine Corps Total Force System (MCTFS). Master record holding duty status, BCP status, and MCC coding.
- UD-MIPS. Cuts duty status transactions per Appendix E.
- Disability Evaluation System (IDES) Workflow. Per Chapter 8 Section 2.
- MTF Records. Hold medical chits and LIMDU board packages.
- OMPF. Holds the disability tab on PLD and EPLD personnel.
- Unit LIMDU Roster. Tracks duty status, re-evaluation gates, and medical scope.

### Forms

- Medical Officer Chit. Per paragraph 8103 (Light Duty).
- LIMDU Board Package. Per paragraphs 8102 and 8104.
- MTF Commander LIMDU Determination Letter. Per paragraph 8104.
- PEB Unfit Findings and CMC (MMSR-4) PLD or EPLD Approval. Per DODI 1332.18 and paragraphs 8108 and 8109.
- Medical Extension Recommendation. Per paragraph 8111.
- Re-Evaluation Appointment Memorandum. Per paragraph 8110.
- Fit-for-Full-Duty Return Memorandum. Local format.

## Common pitfalls

- LIMDU period set past 12 months cumulative without a MEB to the PEB. Per paragraphs 8003.24 and 8102, enlisted TLD runs up to 12 months at the MTF.
- MTF TLD recommendation or extension approval missing. Per paragraphs 8110.2.a and 8111.1.a, the MTF recommends TLD and the commanding officer approves the first extension.
- Re-evaluation gate missed. Per paragraph 8110, the unit and the IPAC track the gate.
- Duty restrictions not applied per medical scope. The medical officer chit and the LIMDU board package govern.
- Marine on LIMDU deployed. Per paragraph 8107.1, the commanding officer decides TLD deployability after weighing the medical officer recommendation.
- PCS executed on a Marine on TLD. Per paragraph 8107.2, Marines on TLD do not execute PCS orders unless the 8107.2 conditions are met.
- LIMDU period or re-evaluation gate missing from the unit tracker. MCO P1070.12K prescribes no LIMDU Page 11 entry, so the tracker carries both.
- MCTFS transaction skipped on entry, change, or closure. Per Appendix E and MCTFSPRIUM, every action cuts a transaction.
- DES referral skipped on PEB-warranting findings. Per Chapter 8 Section 2, PEB-warranting cases refer to DES.
- PLD or EPLD applied without PEB findings. Per paragraphs 8108 and 8109 and Section 2, the PEB drives PLD and EPLD eligibility.

## Decision points

- Light duty versus LIMDU. Per paragraphs 8103 and 8104, the medical officer's expected recovery time drives the path. Short-duration runs through light duty. Longer recovery runs through LIMDU.
- TLD period versus contract extension. Per paragraphs 8102 and 8111, TLD runs in up to two 6 month periods. The medical extension extends the contract to cover the TLD period.
- LIMDU continuation versus DES referral. Per Chapter 8 Section 2, DES applies when the medical condition warrants PEB review. The MTF medical officer and the MTF commander weigh.
- PLD versus EPLD. Per paragraphs 8108 and 8109, the breadth of the medical restrictions and the CMC (MMSR-4) approval drive the classification.
- PFT authorization scope. Per paragraph 8106, the medical officer authorizes partial or no PFT per the medical scope.
- PCS exception during LIMDU. Per paragraph 8107.2, a Marine on TLD executes PCS orders only when the 8107.2 conditions are met.
