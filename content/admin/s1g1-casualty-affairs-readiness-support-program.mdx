## Role and responsibility

The Readiness Support Program (RSP) is a Reserve structure, not a unit S-1 program. Per MCO 3040.4<Citation source="MCO 3040.4" /> Chapter 4 Section 6 paragraph 2.b, RSP provides a trained pool of personnel to conduct casualty notification and assistance during and after mobilization of the Reserve Establishment. RSP personnel serve in a Peacetime/Wartime Support Team (PWST). The S-1 does not activate or run RSP. The S-1 routes casualty assistance to the base or station nearest the next of kin (NOK), or to the PWST, and keeps MFPC informed.

Source documents flow from the casualty case file, NOK location data, mobilization orders, and MFPC tasking. Outputs include the assistance routing record, the case file entry, and MFPC advisories on volume.

Per paragraph 2.a, RSP gives the Marine Corps a presence in local communities and contributes to the overall readiness of the Reserve Establishment. Per paragraph 3, Marine Corps bases and stations assume responsibility for the NOK residing in the geographic area of the base or station. PWST personnel assume duties from the inspector-instructor staffs. The order does not assign RSP responsibility at the unit level.

## Trigger events

- Mobilization of Reserve units with casualties.
- Casualty with PNOK or SNOK residing near a base or station.
- Casualty volume above the capacity of the handling activity.
- Transition from peacetime to wartime casualty assistance.
- MFPC tasking of the nearest Marine Corps activity.

## Processing workflow

1. Confirm the mobilization context. Per MCO 3040.4<Citation source="MCO 3040.4" /> Chapter 4 Section 6 paragraph 1, casualty assistance continues under the order during and after mobilization.
2. Locate the NOK. Identify the base or station nearest the PNOK and SNOK.
3. Route the assistance. Personnel assigned to the base or station provide it, or RSP personnel assigned to a PWST. DC M&RA (MF) tasks other activities as needed.
4. Assign the CACO. Assignments follow the procedures in the order, per paragraph 3.a. Coordinate through MFPC.
5. Advise MFPC on volume. Per paragraph 3.c, an activity unable to handle multiple casualties advises MFPC immediately. MFPC then tasks the nearest activity for personnel and assistance.
6. Document the assistance. Capture each action in the casualty case file.
7. Coordinate with LTAP. Where the case moves to long-term assistance, hand off to the LTAP representative.

## Systems of record and forms

### Systems

- DCIPS. National-level casualty case tracking.
- MFPC (HQMC Casualty Section). Tasks the nearest activity and receives volume advisories.
- Casualty case file. Local record of assistance routing.

### Forms

- MCO 3040.4 Chapter 4 Section 6 prescribes no RSP-specific form. Casualty reporting forms and the CACO Guide apply.

## Common pitfalls

- S-1 treats RSP as a unit program. Per MCO 3040.4<Citation source="MCO 3040.4" /> Chapter 4 Section 6, RSP is a Reserve pool for mobilization. Route to the base, station or PWST.
- NOK in another area handled by the reporting unit. Bases and stations assume responsibility for NOK in their geographic area. Route there.
- Volume exceeds local capacity with no MFPC advisory. Per paragraph 3.c, advise MFPC immediately.
- Coordination with LTAP broken. The case transitions to LTAP without a handoff. Coordinate at transition.

## Decision points

- Who assists the NOK. The base or station nearest the NOK, or RSP personnel in a PWST, per paragraph 1.
- Capacity. Multiple casualties beyond local capacity go to MFPC for tasking, per paragraph 3.c.
- Long-term continuation. Route to HQMC LTAP when the case moves past immediate assistance.
