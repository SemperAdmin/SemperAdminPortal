## Use This Page When

An Equal Opportunity Advisor forwards you a complaint, a Marine brings you a discrimination or harassment allegation, or a delayed favorable personnel action is sitting behind an MEO case. The trigger is the report reaching you, not the outcome.

Your first decision is which lane the report arrived in. Get the lane wrong and every clock below runs on the wrong case.

<Callout variant="warning" title="A confidential report is not yours to investigate">
Under MARADMIN 380/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 380/26" /> paragraph 2.c, a confidential report produces no command notification, inquiry, or investigation. Three exceptions release it: a specific and imminent threat of serious bodily harm or death, disclosed intent to commit a future crime or fraud, and disclosure required by federal law, Department of War policy, or competent legal authority. Nothing else. Opening an inquiry on a confidential report defeats the reporting option and exposes the Marine who used it.
</Callout>

## The Five Clocks

Log the date of receipt in writing the day the complaint reaches you. Every deadline below keys off a date someone will ask you to prove.

| Action | Clock | Owner | Authority |
|--------|-------|-------|-----------|
| Forward a formal complaint to the commander | 5 calendar days from receipt | EOA | Para 5.b |
| Determine dismissal or investigation | 30 calendar days from receipt | You | Para 5.c |
| Written notice of dismissal to the complainant | 7 calendar days from your determination | You | Para 5.d |
| Appeal a dismissal to the next GCMCA | 5 calendar days from the written determination | Complainant | Para 6.a |
| Resume or complete a delayed favorable personnel action | 7 calendar days from dismissal or an unsubstantiated finding | You | Para 7 |

<Callout variant="warning" title="Two clock defects to manage locally">
Paragraph 5.c starts the 30-day clock at receipt without naming whose receipt. Read against the 5-day EOA forwarding duty, the outside window shifts by five days. Run the tighter reading, count from EOA receipt, and you stay compliant under either interpretation.

Paragraph 6.a gives the complainant 5 calendar days to appeal from the written determination, and paragraph 5.d allows you 7 calendar days to deliver it. Delivering notice on day 7 hands the complainant an expired appeal window. Deliver the written notice inside 48 hours and the problem disappears.
</Callout>

## Dismissal Without Investigation

You hold the authority to dismiss a formal MEO complaint without investigation. Consult the command Equal Opportunity Advisor and the staff judge advocate first. Both consultations are required by paragraph 5.a, not optional staffing.

Eight grounds, and no others.

1. Untimely. Filed more than 60 calendar days after the most recent incident. 120 calendar days for a Reserve member.
2. The allegation falls outside the scope of MEO policy.
3. The complaint lacks sufficient or actionable information.
4. Alternative dispute resolution or other informal means resolved the matter.
5. The complainant requests withdrawal.
6. The complainant fails to respond to requests for information.
7. The complaint duplicates another.
8. The complaint constitutes a false official statement under Article 107, UCMJ.

Ground one is a ground for dismissal, not an automatic bar. A late complaint still reaches you and still requires the consultation and the written decision.

Ground eight names an Article 107 exposure. Naming it is not a charging decision. Route any false official statement question to your SJA before it touches the dismissal memo.

## Appeals

The complainant appeals your dismissal to the next higher commander holding General Court-Martial Convening Authority, within 5 calendar days of your written determination. This is an appellate role your GCMCA holds outside the UCMJ referral chain. Paragraph 6.b puts the detailed appeal procedure into the next revision of MCO 5354.1, so expect the mechanics to firm up in the H revision.

State the appeal right and the deadline inside the written notice. A complainant learning about the route after the window closes has a legitimate grievance against your process, not against the outcome.

## Favorable Personnel Actions

Paragraph 7 gives you 7 calendar days to resume or complete any favorable personnel action delayed by the complaint, counted from dismissal or from an unsubstantiated finding. Promotion, orders, awards, schools, and reenlistment all sit in this category.

Pull the list of delayed actions on the day you make the determination. Waiting for the S-1 to notice costs the Marine time you already owe back.

## What You Do Not Do

- Do not investigate a confidential report. Three exceptions release it and nothing else.
- Do not process an anonymous allegation of discrimination as an MEO complaint. Act on the information through another authority instead.
- Do not treat the MEO program as an investigative body. MARADMIN 357/26<Citation source="MARADMIN 357/26" /> states plainly it is non-investigative and not the lead for misconduct outside its scope.
- Do not force conflict resolution. Participation is voluntary for both parties under paragraph 4.c.
- Do not dismiss without both the EOA and the SJA consultation on the record.

## Common Failure Modes

1. Confidential read as anonymous. The two arrive in adjacent paragraphs with opposite effects. Confidential preserves the Marine's identity for authorized personnel and preserves the right to convert. Anonymous produces no complaint at all.
2. The 30-day clock started at the wrong desk. Log the EOA receipt date and the command receipt date separately.
3. Written notice issued without the appeal route. The complainant loses a 5-day window they never learned about.
4. Favorable actions left in limbo after an unsubstantiated finding. The 7-day restart applies to unsubstantiated findings, not to dismissals alone.
5. Conflict resolution treated as a gate. A complainant declining conflict resolution moves straight into the complaint process under paragraph 4.b.
